# Team responsibilities and capacity

| Role | Accountable focus | Coding and support |
|---|---|---|
| Project Director (Development Director) | Backlog, client contact, coordination, attendance, reports, acceptance and submissions | Small frontend/copy work when coordination is covered |
| Technical Director | Architecture approval, hosting, integration and quality | Implements backend/complex pieces; reviews and mentors |
| Senior Developer 1 | Frontend feature delivery | Main coder for shell/planner/map; mentors Junior 1 |
| Senior Developer 2 | Domain/data feature delivery | Main coder for state/rules/data; mentors Junior 2 |
| Junior Developer 1 | Small UI/documentation/QA tasks | Pairs with Senior 1; one narrow task at a time |
| Junior Developer 2 | Source transcription/fixtures/documentation/QA | Pairs with Senior 2; no independent academic rule approval |

The course allows CSCI4691 directors to act as Product Owners/Scrum Masters and developers. Proposed split: PD leads product priority and ceremonies; TD leads technical decisions and helps remove engineering blockers. Confirm titles with the instructor. PD accepts user outcomes; TD approves architecture; another developer reviews code, including TD-authored code.

## Capacity proposal — discuss before committing

Planning starting point: **5 hours per person per week**, not a course requirement or a promise. That gives 30 team hours/week. Deduct about 8 hours for coordination, reviews, mentoring and ceremonies; reserve about 6 for learning, defects and interruptions. Start with about **16 hours/week of new implementation/document production** and adjust using actual progress.

PD's 5 hours may be mostly coordination. TD should protect 2–3 coding hours plus review time. Each senior protects a short junior pairing session and review time; junior tasks should normally fit 1–3 focused hours, with support. A story larger than 8 hours of focused work should be split. Backlog estimates are initial effort bands and may span multiple people; sum dependencies and subtasks before making a sprint commitment. Do not use story estimates as peer grades or hours worked.

At sprint planning ask everyone for available hours, subtract meetings/mentoring, reserve 20% of remaining time, then select stories within the remainder. Confirm holidays, exams and reading-week absences rather than assuming continuous availability during the five-week Sprint 4 window.

If a junior becomes unavailable, prioritize their learning and reduce the source/documentation batch size. Assign uncovered work to a willing senior/TD only after reducing other commitments.

## Working agreement proposal

- Two 15-minute standups weekly, plus brief Teams updates when blocked.
- Raise a blocker after about 20–30 minutes of being stuck; a screenshot/error and what was tried helps the mentor.
- One short weekly backlog/review session; sprint review and retrospective at each milestone.
- Review small PRs within one working day when possible. No self-approval for critical changes.
- Attendance log records availability, absence and follow-up only; no medical diagnosis. Keep peer feedback restricted to the approved course process.
- If ahead, offer to review/test or help a teammate. If behind, reduce or reassign scope early.
